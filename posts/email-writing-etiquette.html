Email Writing Etiquette: Write Emails That Get Replies

October 6, 2026 · By BrightGuide Daily Team

Email is still the language of formal work, yet most emails are harder to read than they need to be. These habits fix that.

The Subject Line Is the Email

Write a subject that states the topic and the need, like: Invoice for March — approval needed by Friday. Vague subjects like 'Hello' or 'Important' get opened last.

One Email, One Topic

Mixing three requests in one message guarantees one of them is forgotten. Send separate short emails for separate topics.

Short Paragraphs, Clear Request

State what you need, by when, in the first three lines. Background details come after, for those who want them.

Tone and Timing

Read the email once as the receiver before sending. Angry emails should wait an hour; urgent emails should be a phone call instead.

End Politely and Clearly

Close with your name, role and phone number. Make replying to you the easiest action in the reader's day, and replies will come faster.
